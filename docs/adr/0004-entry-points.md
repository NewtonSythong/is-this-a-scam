# Three Entry Points: text-selection menu, share sheet, quick-settings tile — and no reading of SMS or notifications

The text-selection menu (`ACTION_PROCESS_TEXT`) is the primary Entry Point because highlighting a message and tapping "Is this a scam?" is the shortest gesture available and needs no permissions; it does not, however, appear when a link is long-pressed, since that menu belongs to the browser, which is why the share sheet is also built. The quick-settings tile is last and cannot read the clipboard on its own — from Android 10 the clipboard is readable only by an app with focus — so the tile launches the app, which then has focus and can pre-fill. Reading SMS directly was rejected because Play policy permits `READ_SMS` only for the device's default SMS handler and forbids even declaring it otherwise. Automatically scanning notifications was rejected because `NotificationListenerService` is a Play-restricted permission granted on the explicit expectation that notification content is never transmitted, and transmitting it to the Narrative Check is precisely what this app would do. All three Entry Points are thin activities over one shared Check function, so the third costs hours rather than days.

`ACTION_PROCESS_TEXT` is documented from Android 6.0 onward, but its behaviour on Android 15 and 16 has not yet been confirmed against the official release notes or a real device — that verification is a prerequisite before it is treated as the primary Entry Point.
