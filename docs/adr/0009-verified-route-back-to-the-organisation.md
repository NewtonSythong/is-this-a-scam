# Every Check points the Checker at a channel the Message had no hand in choosing

A scam supplies its own proof. The number to ring and the link to click both lead back to the person running it, so a Checker who rings "the bank" to confirm is confirming with the scammer, and the confirmation is what convinces them. Breaking that loop is worth more than any single Signal, so where a Known Organisation is implicated, a Check returns advice on how to confirm independently — and every version of that advice ends by telling the Checker not to use any number or link from the Message.

The advice is ordered by what each channel can actually establish. An organisation's own verification feature, where one exists — a bank app that can say whether a call really came from its call centre — is preferred, because it can confirm a specific contact was genuine rather than merely giving the Checker somewhere safe to ask. A verified public phone number is next. Where neither has been confirmed, the advice points at a number the Checker already possesses, from a letter, a bill, or the organisation's app; it never guesses. Both `phone` and `verifiedRoute` on a Known Organisation are nullable for exactly this reason, and are populated only from the organisation's own published material — directing a frightened Checker to a verification feature that does not exist, or to a number that is wrong, is worse than saying nothing.

What this deliberately is not: the app never contacts the sender, and never contacts the organisation on the Checker's behalf. Automated outreach to the sender is engagement with attacker infrastructure, already ruled out by ADR 0008; automated outreach to the organisation would put the app in the middle of the very conversation the Checker needs to have through a channel they trust. The app's job is to hand them the right door, not to walk through it for them.
